# RF return and antenna model requirements

The native audit samples In1 as 3V3 and B.Cu as GND under the antenna-feed
centerline. It does not establish the actual return-current path. Keep these
as distinct nets; do not relabel or short the power plane to ground merely to
make a simulation pass.

The native footprint inventory found no footprint with direct `/3V3` and
`/GND` pads within 30 mm of the sampled feed segment. It did find nearby
ground-referenced capacitors on other nets, including C115 on the radio's
`VDD_CC119` net. See `return_candidates.json` and `near_ground_caps.json`;
these are geometric/net inventories, not evidence that components are
populated, or that a particular RF return path is continuous.

For the next board model:

1. Expand the imported region far enough to include the real ground and power
   planes, the relevant stitching and component vias, and the actual bypass
   connections between 3V3 and GND. Include component and via impedance over
   the modeled band; identify any missing values or geometry as assumptions.
2. Place ports at defined launch reference planes with explicit signal and
   intended return terminals. Check their orientation and extraction against
   a matched through-line before interpreting a board-port power balance.
3. Include the U.FL launch and either a documented antenna model with complex
   impedance or a clearly stated 50-ohm termination. Model the intended
   enclosure and feed cable when assessing the installed antenna. Nominal
   impedance or a return-loss magnitude alone does not specify phase.
4. Apply open boundaries to the live solver, document domain size and material
   models, and evaluate time and mesh sensitivity. The saved PML retry is a
   diagnostic of the cropped geometry, not a substitute for these steps.

These are simulation requirements, not authorization for physical testing.
Even a validated linear model of the complete passive structure would not by
itself establish harmonics generated by the powered RF chain.
